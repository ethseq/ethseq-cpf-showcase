# Collaboration

## Who we are looking to work with

EthSeq is looking for:

- robot manipulation teams;
- embodied AI and VLA teams;
- policy-evaluation researchers;
- teams with reproducible policy failures; and
- external validation partners.

## Proposed collaboration format

The goal is to test robot-policy failure attribution in a partner’s own setting, rather than to ask a partner to reproduce an EthSeq benchmark.

1. **Bring a reproducible failure.** The partner provides its own task, policy or model, data, and execution environment as appropriate.
2. **Define the bounded test space.** Together, the parties define what can be observed and controlled for the validation question.
3. **Run the attribution workflow.** CPF discovers a candidate inside that space, freezes it before the final experiment, and evaluates controlled intervention evidence.
4. **Accept the evidence outcome.** The result can be **PASS**, **FAIL**, or **ABSTAIN**. The collaboration does not require a result that favors EthSeq.

The exact data-access, execution, confidentiality, and intellectual-property arrangements are determined per collaboration.

## What a partner can evaluate

A partner can evaluate whether CPF helps answer a practical question that is otherwise difficult to resolve:

> Did this specific factor actually cause the observed robot-policy failure?

The study can also establish a useful negative result: the tested factor did not receive support, or the available evidence was insufficient for attribution.
